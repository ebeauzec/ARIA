# Data Protection — SnapMirror / SnapVault / SnapCenter / MetroCluster

## SnapMirror & SnapVault (ONTAP-native)
- [Vault/archive using SnapMirror technology — concept](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/data-protection/vault-archive-snapmirror-technology-concept.html)
- Key distinction: SnapVault retains Snapshot copies on the secondary even after primary deletion (up to the ONTAP 255-copy ceiling); SnapMirror does not retain copies once deleted on the source.
- SnapMirror policies support up to 10 retention rules per policy.
- **SnapMirror Synchronous — two policy types, verified 2026-07-09**: available since ONTAP 9.5 on FAS/AFF systems with 16GB+ RAM (SnapMirror-license-gated). Configured with one of two policies, not a single "sync mode" — `sync-mirror`: on an `OutOfSync` event, primary I/O **continues uninterrupted** (non-disruptive, but the destination falls behind until resync); `strict-sync-mirror`: on an `OutOfSync` event, primary I/O **stops** until the relationship is restored (zero-RPO enforced at the cost of availability). Neither is deprecated. Treat the choice between them as a deliberate availability-vs-consistency design decision under the [Operating Protocol](../OPERATING-PROTOCOL.md#2-classify-every-recommended-action-before-giving-steps), not a "stricter is safer" default. Full version-history context: [ONTAP-Core/CLI-Version-History.md](../ONTAP-Core/CLI-Version-History.md).
- **ARP/AI on synchronous primaries (AFX, ONTAP 9.19.1, found 2026-07-30)**: Autonomous Ransomware Protection with AI now supports eligible primary volumes in a SnapMirror synchronous relationship on AFX systems — runs on the active read-write primary and creates primary-only ARP snapshots (machine-learning-based ransomware detection). Source: [What's new in ONTAP 9.19.1 for AFX storage systems](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap-afx/release-notes/whats-new-9191.html); see [Platforms-Hardware — AFX](../Platforms-Hardware/README.md#afx--ontap-9191-feature-release-notes-new-page-discovered-2026-07-30-dated-05112026) for the full feature list from the same page, and [Security-Hardening](../Security-Hardening/README.md) for the general ARP/MAV framework this extends.
- **Two additional SnapMirror synchronous capabilities, ONTAP 9.19.1 — found 2026-09-17, direct-fetch confirmed** ([What's new in ONTAP 9.19.1](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/release-notes/whats-new-9191.html), core ONTAP page — this closes a library gap rather than reflecting a new NetApp release, since the page itself is unchanged since 07/28/2026): (1) **tamperproof snapshot locking** is now supported with SnapMirror synchronous — pairs the [SnapLock](#snaplock--compliancworm) WORM guarantee with a zero-RPO synchronous relationship rather than only an async SnapMirror/SnapVault target; (2) SnapMirror synchronous now supports **replicating scheduled snapshots to the destination volume**, rather than only the synchronously-mirrored data itself. Neither changes the `sync-mirror`/`strict-sync-mirror` policy behavior already documented above.

**Risk & disruption notes (see [OPERATING-PROTOCOL.md](../OPERATING-PROTOCOL.md) for the full framework):**
- `snapmirror break` — **destructive/irreversible for the relationship state**: converts the destination to a writable, independent copy and ends ongoing replication. Data itself is not deleted, but the protection relationship is gone until explicitly re-established (`resync`), and any writes made to the broken-off destination create divergence that `resync` will discard unless handled deliberately. Confirm the relationship is healthy and fully synced (`snapmirror show` — check lag/status) before breaking it, and confirm *why* it's being broken (planned failover/test vs. DR) before proceeding.
- `snapmirror resync` after a break — can discard data written to whichever side is not chosen as the resync source. State explicitly which side's data will be kept and which will be overwritten before running it.
- `snapmirror delete` / removing a policy's retention rules — **destructive**: deletes the relationship and, depending on retention settings, may allow orphaned Snapshot copies on the secondary to be freed. Verify no other recovery path depends on that relationship first.
- Routine `snapmirror update` and creating new relationships/policies are **non-disruptive**.

## SnapDiff v3 & SnapMirror Cloud — NAS backup-to-object architecture
**New section, added 2026-07-11.** [A new backup architecture for NAS systems — SnapDiff V3 (NetApp blog)](https://www.netapp.com/blog/new-backup-architecture-snapdiff-v3/) · [FAQ: SnapDiff support in ONTAP (KB)](https://kb.netapp.com/on-prem/ontap/DP/SnapDiff/SnapDiff-KBs/FAQ_SnapDiff_Support_in_ONTAP). This is the NetApp-native mechanism underneath several third-party backup integrations already documented in [Backup-3rdParty](../Backup-3rdParty/README.md) (Veeam's SnapDiff-API-based CFT, Rubrik's SnapDiff-API access) — worth citing directly when a customer asks how these vendors achieve fast NAS incrementals without a filesystem crawl.
- **SnapDiff**: a differencing engine that generates the list of changed files between two Snapshot copies of a volume — the mechanism a backup application uses to avoid a full filesystem crawl on every incremental backup. Available since ONTAP 9.8 (v3); confirm the exact version/API surface a given third-party product requires against its own release notes, since backup vendors have historically staggered their SnapDiff API adoption (see the Veeam CFT version caveats already logged under Backup-3rdParty).
- **SnapMirror Cloud**: an extension of SnapMirror that transfers Snapshot copies from an ONTAP system directly to an object storage target (S3-compatible, including StorageGRID or a hyperscaler bucket) rather than to another ONTAP system.
- **Combined architecture**: SnapDiff identifies the changed-file delta between Snapshots; SnapMirror Cloud transfers only that delta to object storage. Properties: **forever-incremental** (one initial full transfer, no repeated full backups thereafter), **block-level incremental** (a single changed block within a file moves only that block, not the whole file), **storage-efficiency-preserving** (ONTAP-side dedupe/compression carries through to the object-storage copy rather than being re-inflated), and **granular file-level recovery** directly from the object-storage copy.
- **Design relevance**: this is the architecture a customer is implicitly relying on when a third-party backup vendor advertises "NetApp SnapDiff API support" — cite this section alongside the vendor-specific entry in [Backup-3rdParty](../Backup-3rdParty/README.md) rather than treating the vendor's integration as a black box.

## SnapLock — compliance/WORM
**New section, added 2026-07-11.** [Learn about ONTAP SnapLock](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/snaplock/) · [Archive and compliance using SnapLock technology (PDF)](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/pdfs/sidebar/Archive_and_compliance_using_SnapLock_technology.pdf) · [Configure SnapLock (PDF)](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/pdfs/sidebar/Configure_SnapLock.pdf) · [Commit snapshots to WORM on a vault destination](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/snaplock/commit-snapshot-copies-worm-concept.html) · [Initialize the Compliance Clock](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap/snaplock/initialize-complianceclock-task.html) · [SnapLock feature in ONTAP 9 (KB)](https://kb.netapp.com/on-prem/ontap/DP/SnapLock/SnapLock-KBs/SnapLock_feature_in_ONTAP_9) · TR-4526 — Compliant WORM storage using NetApp SnapLock ([PDF](https://www.netapp.com/media/6158-tr4526.pdf)).
- **What it is**: an integrated hardware-and-software WORM (write-once, read-many) solution — files committed to a SnapLock volume become non-erasable/non-rewritable for a designated retention period (or indefinitely), enforced at the file level over standard NFS/CIFS. The integrated hardware+software combination matters specifically for strict regulatory frameworks (SEC Rule 17a-4(f)) that require it, not a software-only WORM claim.
- **Two licensed modes**: **Compliance** mode — meets strict external mandates (SEC 17a-4(f), HIPAA, FINRA, CFTC, GDPR retention provisions); once committed, data cannot be deleted before the retention period expires by *any* administrator, including NetApp Support. **Enterprise** mode — meets internally-mandated retention policy; a privileged administrator retains an emergency override path to delete before expiry, intended for internal governance rather than regulator-facing compliance.
- **SnapLock for SnapVault**: WORM-protects Snapshot copies replicated to secondary/DR storage, not just the primary volume — relevant when the compliance requirement is "an immutable copy must exist off the primary system," not just "the primary copy must be immutable."
- **Compliance Clock**: SnapLock Compliance mode relies on a tamper-resistant clock independent of the system clock — must be initialized once per node before any Compliance-mode volume can be created; this is a one-way, irreversible initialization. Flag this as a genuinely one-time, no-undo step under the [Operating Protocol](../OPERATING-PROTOCOL.md) before a customer runs it, distinct from the retention-period commitments themselves.
- **Risk note**: a file/volume committed under SnapLock Compliance mode **cannot be shortened or deleted early by design** — this is the feature working as intended, not a bug to work around. Before recommending a retention period or Compliance-mode commitment, confirm the customer understands there is no emergency-override or NetApp-Support-assisted early-deletion path in Compliance mode (unlike Enterprise mode) — get the retention period and mode choice right before the first commit, not after.

**Risk & disruption notes:**
- **Planned switchover** (`metrocluster switchover`) — **disruptive but data-safe when pre-conditions hold**: briefly interrupts I/O during the transition and requires the surviving site's mirrored aggregates to be in sync first. Before recommending it, confirm via `metrocluster check run`/`metrocluster check show` (or the equivalent for the ONTAP version in scope) that there are no unmirrored aggregates, no existing site-down condition, and no in-progress resync — switching over against an unhealthy mirror can strand data instead of protecting it. Always pair the switchover procedure with the documented [switchback](https://docs.netapp.com/us-en/ontap-metrocluster/manage/task_perform_switchover_for_tests_or_maintenance.html) steps and don't leave a cluster running in switched-over state longer than the maintenance window requires.
- **Unplanned/forced switchover** (declaring a disaster) — treat as an emergency escalation, not a routine recommendation; confirm the outage is genuinely site-level before invoking it, since a forced switchover on a false alarm can itself cause disruption.
- **Healing and switchback** after a switchover — sequence-sensitive; performing switchback before the disaster site's storage/controllers are confirmed healthy again risks re-introducing the original fault. Follow the documented healing → switchback order, don't skip ahead.
- **Expanding, converting (FC↔IP), or re-cabling a MetroCluster** — physically disruptive by nature and vendor/switch-firmware sensitive; always cross-check the specific platform's guide (linked above and in [Host-Integration-Cabling](../Host-Integration-Cabling/README.md)) rather than generalizing from a different MetroCluster generation.
- Routine health checks (`metrocluster show`, `metrocluster check run` in read/report mode) are **non-disruptive** and should be the first step recommended whenever MetroCluster health is in question — before any change is proposed.
